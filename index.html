01

RAPIDO · CHRISTMAS CAMPAIGN

Turning a customer friction into a Christmas surprise.

The problem

Rapido had a real customer friction in Chennai. Some auto drivers were asking customers to pay above the fare shown on the app, leaving customers unhappy with the overall experience.

For Christmas, the brand wanted an interactive campaign that could rebuild some of that warmth.

The idea

I wanted the interaction to happen where the problem happened: inside the auto ride itself.

What if your Rapido driver was Santa?

A Santa would drive unsuspecting customers and surprise them with a curated Christmas goodie box at the end of the ride.

The creative call

I identified and coordinated with a celebrity known for family-friendly comedy and strong audience connect to play Santa. Customers discovered who he was only at the end of the ride. Their genuine reactions became the campaign content.

02

RAACK ACADEMY · BRAND POSITIONING

From a dance academy to something parents put on the curriculum.

The problem

With 30+ franchises, Raack already had recognition within the dance fraternity. The next challenge was to move beyond people already interested in dance and reach a wider commercial audience.

The positioning

I proposed positioning dance not simply as an extracurricular activity, but as part of a child's growing years.

Don't sell dance classes.
Build a case for dance education.

Founder-led communication began speaking about the developmental edge of early dance education.

The audience shift

I focused the communication on young parents and parents of toddlers. A child entering Raack at 3 could potentially continue until 15 or 16, so reaching parents earlier also created the possibility of a much longer brand relationship.

03

K.R. NAGARAJ · PERSONAL BRANDING

Taking an established name offline and making it relevant online.

The challenge

K.R. Nagaraj already had decades of business credibility offline. Entering digital meant speaking to a younger generation that hadn't necessarily grown up knowing his journey.

The obvious route was to lead with success. I chose not to.

The strategy

I positioned his early content around the parts of the journey people rarely hear about: failures, career struggles, setbacks, decisions and lessons.

The success established his credibility.
The struggle made a new generation listen.

The audience thinking

The goal wasn't to make KRN look younger for social media. It was to identify what a younger audience could genuinely take away from someone with his experience. His career stories became that bridge.

Personal Brand PositioningAudience StrategyContent DirectionFounder StorytellingDigital Launch StrategyContent Planning

Personal branding isn't always about polishing the success story. Sometimes, the rough chapters are the brand.

04

SUNDARI SILKS · FESTIVE 2026

360° marketing. But not 360° for the sake of being everywhere.

The brief

I worked on Sundari Silks' 2026 festive marketing plan across traditional, digital and in-store touchpoints. The collection leaned vintage and culturally rooted, while the core customer sat within a focused premium audience.

The media thinking

I mapped the campaign around where that premium audience was most relevant rather than giving every medium equal weight. Print included The Hindu. Physical OOH placements were selected around premium residential catchments. Influencers, creators, digital and in-store visibility followed the same audience lens.

The principle

Reach mattered.
Relevant reach mattered more.

The objective wasn't to put Sundari everywhere. It was to make every place we put Sundari make sense.

05

SAREGAMA SOUTH · MULTI-MARKET STRATEGY

Four languages. Multiple labels. And the release calendar rarely took a day off.

The scale

Across four regional language labels, songs were releasing almost every day, each with its own content, audience and promotional requirements. Larger releases added artists, influencers, promotional content and paid media to the mix.

What I handled

I managed social media and release communication across Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam and Kannada. For regular releases, I handled release planning, social communication and platform execution. Priority releases received a wider promotion plan involving artists, influencers and paid support.

The strategy

The volume was high, but volume couldn't become the strategy. A catalogue release, a new film song and a major artist release didn't deserve identical promotional treatment.

When something releases every day, everything can't be treated like the biggest release of the day.
